Cutting Your AWS Bill by 60% — Without Downsizing

When our AWS bill crossed $400K/month we didn't fire anyone or shut anything off. We just stopped paying for things we weren't using. Here's the playbook.

1. Reserved Instances + Savings Plans

For predictable workloads, commit for 1–3 years. Savings of 30–60% over on-demand. We moved 80% of steady-state EC2 and RDS to Savings Plans.

2. Right-sizing

Use Compute Optimizer and Trusted Advisor. Half our instances were oversized by 2x. Downgrading saved us $42K/month with zero impact.

3. S3 lifecycle policies

Move logs older than 30 days to Glacier. Delete them after 90. We cut S3 spend by 70%.

4. Spot instances for batch jobs

Anything fault-tolerant runs on Spot. We saved 80% on our CI build fleet.

5. NAT gateway reduction

NAT gateways are deceptively expensive. We replaced 14 NAT gateways with a single VPC endpoint setup using PrivateLink. Saved $11K/month.

6. Data transfer out

CloudFront in front of S3 cut our bandwidth costs by 60%. Data transfer OUT is where AWS makes its real money — caching is your defense.

7. Tag everything

Without tags you can't attribute spend. We use a tag policy that auto-rejects untagged resources. Visibility came first, savings followed.

Cloud cost optimization isn't a one-time audit. It's a continuous practice. Build a FinOps culture, give engineers dashboards, and watch the bill drop.
