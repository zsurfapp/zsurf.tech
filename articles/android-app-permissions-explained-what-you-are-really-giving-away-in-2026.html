Android App Permissions Explained: What You Are Really Giving Away

Android app permissions control what apps can access on your phone. Most users accept every permission request without reading them, which is a privacy and security risk. Here is the complete guide to managing app permissions in 2026.

How the permission system works

Android uses a runtime permission model since version 6. Apps request access to a capability when they need it, and you grant or deny. Denied permissions can be re requested. You can revoke any permission at any time in Settings.

The dangerous permissions

Location: tracks where you are. Microphone: records audio. Camera: takes photos and videos. Contacts: reads your address book. Storage: reads files on your phone. SMS: reads and sends text messages. Phone: makes calls. Calendar: reads your schedule.

The one time permissions

Android 11 introduced one time permissions for camera, microphone, and location. Granting one time access means the app has access until you close it. The next time you open it, it asks again.

Permission groups vs individual permissions

Some permissions are grouped: location includes fine, coarse, and background. Camera is a single permission. Microphone is a single permission. The contacts permission includes read and write.

How to audit app permissions

Go to Settings, Privacy, Permission manager. You will see every permission and how many apps have it. Tap a permission to see which apps have it. Revoke access for apps that do not need it.

Common apps that overreach

Flashlight apps requesting location. Calculator apps requesting contacts. Wallpaper apps requesting storage and microphone. Free VPNs requesting everything. If an app requests more than it needs, do not install it.

Auto revoke for unused apps

Android 11 added auto revoke: if you have not used an app in 3 months, its runtime permissions are automatically revoked. Enable this in Settings, Privacy, Permission manager, Auto revoke.
