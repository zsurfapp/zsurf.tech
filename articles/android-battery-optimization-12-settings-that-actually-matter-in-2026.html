Android Battery Optimization: 12 Settings That Actually Matter

Android battery life in 2026 is generally good, but heavy users still struggle to make it through a full day. These are the settings and habits that actually move the needle, ranked by impact.

The biggest battery drain: display

The display is the single biggest drain on most phones. Lowering brightness from 100 percent to 50 percent can double battery life. Enabling dark mode on AMOLED screens saves another 10 to 20 percent. Reducing the refresh rate from 120 hertz to 60 hertz saves 5 to 10 percent.

Background apps are the second biggest drain

Apps running in the background consume significant battery. Go to Settings, Battery, Battery usage to see which apps are draining the most. Force stop or uninstall the worst offenders. Restrict background activity for apps you do not use daily.

Disable 5G when you do not need it

5G uses 20 to 30 percent more battery than 4G for the same workload. If you do not need 5G speeds, switch to 4G only mode in Settings. The difference is most noticeable indoors where the 5G signal is weak.

Turn off always on display

Always on display looks great but uses 5 to 10 percent battery per day. Disable it if you need every bit of battery. The Pixel 10 Pro has a particularly efficient implementation that is barely noticeable.

Manage location services

GPS is a major battery drain. Set location to Device only instead of High accuracy. Restrict which apps can use location in the background. Turn off WiFi and Bluetooth scanning.

Disable haptic feedback

Haptic feedback consumes a small but measurable amount of battery. Disable it for keyboard taps and navigation gestures if you want every bit of life.

Manage push notifications

Every push notification wakes the phone. Disable notifications for apps that do not need to interrupt you. Use Do Not Disturb during sleep hours. Batch notifications from social apps.

The nuclear option: battery saver

Most phones have a battery saver mode that limits background activity, reduces performance, and disables non essential features. Enable it when you hit 20 percent. Modern implementations are aggressive enough to add 4 to 6 hours of life.
