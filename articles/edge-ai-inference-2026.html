Edge AI Inference in 2026: From Cloud to Silicon

The shift of AI inference from cloud to edge has been promised for years, and 2026 is the year it actually arrived. New mobile NPUs deliver 50+ TOPS at single-digit watts, WebGPU is stable in every major browser, and quantization tooling has matured enough that production teams are shipping 7B parameter models to phones. This article is the field guide we wish we had twelve months ago.

The state of edge AI hardware in 2026

Every flagship phone now ships with a dedicated neural processing unit capable of running billion-parameter models. Apple A19 Pro, Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 2, and Tensor G5 all deliver comparable performance, and the question is no longer whether edge inference is feasible but which model fits the budget.

Quantization without regret

INT4 and INT8 quantization is now table stakes, but the interesting developments are in mixed-precision schemes that keep sensitive layers at higher precision. We walk through the toolchain and the failure modes we have observed in production.

WebGPU and the browser as a deployment target

WebGPU is now stable in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari. Models that previously required a 2GB native download can now run in the browser with similar performance. The implications for SaaS cost structures are significant.

The economics of edge deployment

Cloud inference costs roughly 8 to 12 cents per million tokens at the time of writing. Edge inference, amortized over device lifetime, can bring that to fractions of a cent. We model the break-even point for several workload types.
